It goes in three steps (@Pauli, Nov 12):
   1. **Whether the fix is feasible in the map code**, for every output: whether `Map.DrawColor` can draw a pattern once for all the areas that share its frame in a colour's run, and a colour's transparency once for its layer, in each target; how fast the screen then draws a large map; and which baselines move. Done on Nov 13 (0b0e8a8): the design's "Overlapping patterns, and a colour's layer", with the scripts in `SvgHatchingOverlap/`; @Pauli chose (a), the fix in the map code for every output (see Decisions, overlapping patterns).

If the prototype on `proto/overlapping-patterns` already works when step 2 ends, it is taken first, before task 3's upstream sync; otherwise the fix is built after tasks 3 and 4, on the code synced with upstream (@Pauli, Nov 12). It goes in the map code on a `fix/` branch rooted where `base` meets upstream, with the baselines it moves (see "Fixes for Purple Pen"), and the SVG's side on `feature/svg-target`. The requirements' new wording, of M-1 and D-2, and of how the ghosts fit M-6 and S-12, comes to @Pauli with the result. The prototype on `proto/overlapping-patterns` (dbaa918, rooted at 98c168b) is not the fix yet, so tasks 3 and 4 come first: it still needs the SVG's side, the 84 baselines recorded, tests of its own, its measurement switches taken out, each group's lattice drawn once, smoothed edges on the groups a pattern brush fills, patterns that never turn grouped across angles, and the browser canvas's union.
   - **The cause, as the SVG suggests it.** Areas 2 and 3 are unturned, so their lines lie on one grid from the map's origin, and where they overlap every line is drawn twice on itself. A renderer that smooths edges draws a line narrower than a pixel with partial coverage, and blends each copy's coverage on its own, so a pixel row that one line covers by 57 % ends up 81 % covered by two: the band looks thicker. Area 1's hatching is turned 179.9°, about the map's origin some 300 mm away, so its lines lie about 0.065 mm off the others' and do not continue theirs across the edge they share. To be verified in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, in a PDF viewer, and in the application's own drawing.
   - **The PNG export** shows it at 100 dpi (@Pauli, Nov 12), so the application draws an overlap alike; at higher resolutions a line covers whole pixels but at its edges, and the doubling hides. To be measured at several resolutions, with what it means for print.
   - **Ways for overlapping areas of one symbol to blend into one hatching**, with what each costs. @Pauli's condition (Nov 12): merging the areas would make it harder to bind each drawn element to its map object and symbol without ambiguity, so each way is weighed against that binding (M-6), and against @Pauli's ruling of Nov 6 that merging paths is the map maker's business, not a hidden step of the export (see path merging). For example:
     - each object hatches only its own share, clipped to its outline less those of the earlier areas of its symbol, so that every object keeps its own element and number, and the lines meet end to end where one share gives way to the next;
     - the symbol hatches once, under one clip that keeps each object's outline as an element with its number, so that each outline stays bound to its object, and the hatching to its symbol;
     - the merged area is drawn once, and each object's own area is kept over it as an invisible "ghost" that carries the object's number and a reference to the merged area that replaced it (@Pauli, Nov 12). The ghosts can themselves make the merged area's clip, so that each outline is written once, the renderer forms the union, and the binding runs both ways; a browser can also tell which ghost a pointer is over. To check: how Inkscape and Scribus treat invisible elements, how the structure walk (V-7) and the geometry check (V-10) read them, the size they add, and whether a seam stays where two areas only touch;
     - if the fix is not feasible in the map code, the same in the SVG alone.

     @Pauli asked whether the ghosts' clip could be the one way every patterned area is drawn (Nov 12). The investigation answers that too, against these limits:

## Future directions

Undertakings to return to once the SVG work is done (@Pauli, Nov 6 and 12).

- **Flattening blending and opacity.** An option of the export turns every colour drawn with blending or less than full opacity into opaque pieces:
  - It covers the course's purple and the map's own colours, such as contours, whose opacity is set per colour.
  - It is a stage in front of the targets, so that PDF, SVG and printing share it.
  - Each such colour is cut against every colour below it, in the map's stacking order. Each piece is filled with the mixed CMYK, the piece over white included.
  - The pieces are real geometry, made with boolean path operations; SkiaSharp's keep curves. Old printers' PostScript and PDF interpreters take plain filled shapes without transparency or huge clip paths, and editors handle them best.
  - Against hairline seams between pieces, a setting grows each cutting shape by a set distance, about 0.02 to 0.05 mm. A piece then overlaps its neighbour along the cut edges, while its outer edge stays at the object's edge.
  - Map lines are turned into outlines before they cut. Bitmaps under a blended colour cannot be cut as vectors.
  - It covers solid colours with opacity and the standard blend modes, and nothing else.
  - It is designed to be carried to Inkscape and Scribus as well:
    - a short specification of the mixing, worked in CMYK by ISO 32000's transparency rules, and of the cutting;
    - only the geometry that every library offers: outlines, booleans and growth;
    - SVG as the format a standalone flattener reads and writes;
    - a shared test set that any port must pass;
    - a licence that GPL-2.0-or-later programs can take in.
  - The geometry is Skia's path operations, which keep curves rather than reducing them to polygons. Running in the browser is a priority (@Pauli, Nov 6). The operations are available in C++, in C# through SkiaSharp, in Python as `skia-pathops`, and in the browser through CanvasKit, Skia compiled to WebAssembly. Edges that two areas share exactly, which maps are full of, are the input to test them hardest on.
  - The language is still to be chosen, between two shapes:
    - one implementation per platform on the same Skia algorithm: TypeScript on CanvasKit for the browser, beside the TypeScript port (phase 8), C# on SkiaSharp in Purple Pen, and Python on `skia-pathops` for Inkscape and Scribus;
    - a single C++ core on Skia's path operations, compiled to WebAssembly for the browser and natively for the rest.
- **A PDF base map as vectors** (@Pauli, Nov 12; not current yet). The application reads a PDF base map's page with PdfPig into drawing calls, which the target draws as it draws a map, as the design's "A PDF base map as vectors" sets out and as @Pauli chose (see Decisions): with a paint for RGB colours in `Graphics2D`, a JPEG written through as its own data, loop cases of PDF maps, and the bitmap as the fallback for a page the reader cannot draw. It comes with its requirement, in place of the line that leaves it out of scope, and the port's freedom from PDF maps in P-1, put to @Pauli with the result.
- **A colour's own transparency** (@Pauli, Nov 13). Purple Pen reads no colour's transparency, OCAD's `t` and Mapper's `opacity`, and draws such a colour opaque; three colours of the test maps have one. Read, it would make each such colour one layer at its opacity in every output (see Decisions, a colour is a layer), so that the colour's objects do not darken one another where they overlap, as they do in Mapper, which applies it object by object. It changes how maps with transparent colours draw, and their baselines; whether OCAD applies it to the layer as a whole is not known. COL-5 would say where the opacity goes. Flattening needs it.
- **Vector printing.** An option, not the default, at least at first: printing stays rasterized unless the option is chosen.
  - It is built without WinForms.
  - On Linux and macOS, a PDF goes to CUPS: either the PDF export's, or the Skia target's drawing recorded into a PDF, which draws what the raster print draws.
  - On Windows, it goes through Direct2D's print support, or as XPS through Windows' print API.
  - With vectors, the driver or CUPS filter decides the result: CMYK is converted, and blend modes may be rasterized or handled differently. Flattening in front of it leaves drivers no blending to mishandle.

## Decisions

- **The structured interface.** `IStructuredGraphicsTarget`, in `Graphics2D`, extends `IGraphicsTarget`, and @Pauli approved its calls:
  - `BeginPart` and `EndPart` around a part of a drawing, which the document defines once;
  - `BeginMap` and `EndMap` around a labeled map, nested for a template;
  - `AddPage(paperSize, mapArea, mapToPage, partKeys)`, made outside parts;
  - `BeginColorRun(colorKey, position, name, color, overprint)` with `EndColorRun`;
  - `BeginObject(objectKey, symbolKey, symbolId, symbolName)` with `EndObject`;
  - `SetGeoreference(mapKey, MapGeoreference)`, which a later call for the same map replaces.

  Since then, `DescribeCourse` and `DescribeObjects` carry the course data (CD-1, CD-2), `AddPage` takes the page's name last (see phase 5's choices), and `PatternBrushesAreVectors` tells the map code that the target's pattern brushes are vectors (S-7).
- **Shared code.** `IdentityComparer` lives in `Graphics2D`, from where every graphics target, the SVG target too, keys its resources by identity (C-4, C-6).
- **The document.** Parts are groups with ids in `<defs>`. A page is a group carrying the page's transform and a clip of its map area, with a `<use>` for each part it shows. Pages after the first lie to its right, 10 mm apart, and the root has the first page's size in millimetres. The metadata's root is `npp:document version="1"`, holding an `npp:map` with the number and label of each map, whose groups carry `data-m`.
- **Numbers.** Coordinates are whole steps of 0.001 mm, rounded half up as `floor(x + 0.5)`, which every runtime does alike (P-6). A transform carries the digits that the reach of its content needs: within half a step for its translation, and within 0.45 of a step for its linear part, shared between angle (0.2), scale (0.2) and shear (0.05).
- **The text.** Each path command takes its shortest form; arc flags keep their separators, which SVG 1.1 readers need, and transform functions follow each other without one. Ids are named in the order they are first used, the shortest names first. Groups are tidied before writing:
  - empty ones are dropped, and those carrying nothing dissolved;
  - an only child is folded into its parent where the order of transform and clip allows;
  - a group carrying nothing but a translation and holding one ellipse gives way to the ellipse moved into place, its center rounded to whole steps;
  - adjacent groups with equal attributes are merged, unless they blend.

  @Pauli approved the folding of single ellipses, which takes irregular1's document from 298,736 to 220,661 bytes.
- **Colors.** `icc-color()` names the profile `cmyk`, declared as `<color-profile id="cmyk" name="cmyk" xlink:href="data:application/vnd.iccprofile;base64,..."/>`, which holds a 516-byte device-CMYK profile (see colours for Scribus and Inkscape). The sRGB comes from the converter passed in; the comparison passes `DefaultColorConverter`, which converts as the Skia references do.
- **Anti-aliasing.** Browsers anti-alias by default, so a group carries `shape-rendering` only where the mode changes. The export draws with `MapDisplay.AntiAliasWhenPrinting`, with which printing keeps the screen's anti-aliasing and still draws without pattern bitmaps (25d137c): without it, printing pushes `PushAntiAliasing(false)`, which D-9 honours, and the pages drew jagged in browsers and viewers.
- **Errors.** A call the target does not implement yet raises `Svg_UnsupportedCallException`, which makes a comparison case pending; a call the target will never honor as made says why, beginning with the requirement that says so, and its case is pending for good (DL-6); a call out of turn raises an `InvalidOperationException` that names it.
- **Pens.** A pen is a style: `fill:none`, its paint, its width in steps, then its cap and join where they differ from SVG's defaults of butt, miter and a miter limit of 4 (D-5, S-9). Skia bevels every corner under a miter limit of 1 or less, which SVG would take for an error, so such a pen is written with a bevel join. A pen of zero width, Skia's hairline of one device pixel at any scale, has no width in a document: the target leaves out every line drawn with it, and names the calls it left out (D-5, D-12). `MiterClipped` is written as a miter, as PDF export draws it (D-5). A pen made from a brush strokes with the brush's paint (D-6).
- **Shapes.** Lines, polylines, polygons, rectangles and paths are written as paths (`SvgPath`), ellipses as circles or ellipses (`SvgEllipse`). An arc's angles are degrees turning from the x axis towards the y axis, as Skia's are; it is written as an arc command, and an arc of a full turn or more as a circle, as Skia draws it as a closed oval (D-4). A path's parts become its commands, a line or curve with no start before it beginning at the origin as in Skia, and a path with nothing in it draws nothing (D-3).
- **Clips.** A clip is a group with a `clip-path`. The document defines each clip path once for all equal clips, the page's among them, since a clip path is read in the user space of what refers to it. The union of rectangles is one subpath per rectangle, each turning clockwise, under the nonzero rule (D-2).
- **The map's structure.** To a target that implements `IStructuredGraphicsTarget`, `Map.DrawColor` opens a run of a color of the color table before its first object and closes it after its last, and brackets each object with its symbol. The image and layout layers, which are not colors of the table, open no run, so their objects sit in their map outside any run. `Map.DrawTemplates` labels each template with its file name (M-3 to M-5).
  - A run is a group carrying `data-c`, the color's position in its map's color table.
  - Consecutive objects of one symbol share a group carrying `data-s`: the symbol's number in its map's symbol table, counted from 1 in the order the map's symbols first begin an object. A call that closes or begins something around the group closes it, once its last object has ended.
  - Each element carries `data-o`, its object's number, counted across the document from 1 in the order objects are first drawn, so an object drawn in several runs has one number (M-6, S-11).
  - Each `npp:map` holds, in table order, an `npp:color` for each color its runs drew: `position`, `name`, `cmyk` with the digits of `icc-color()`, and `overprint="1"` when it overprints. Then comes an `npp:symbol` for each symbol its objects drew: `n`, `id`, `name`, and `colors`, the positions of the colors its objects were drawn in (M-7).
  - The structure adds about 21 % to the SVG of the 101 drawn cases, most of it the object numbers.
- **Georeferencing.** `MapGeoreference`, a plain value in `Graphics2D`, carries the affine transform from millimetres of map to real-world coordinates and the map's EPSG code, Proj4 string and OCAD grid and zone id (G-1, G-2).
  - A map's record in the metadata opens with `npp:georeference`, ahead of its tables, so a map with a georeference and no tables still has a record.
  - Its `transform` takes steps of map to the real world. It is written as every transform is (S-8), and left out when it moves nothing (S-9). It keeps the digits that hold a point within a step of map over the 8 m the map's coordinates reach (S-2).
  - `epsg`, `proj4` and `ocad-grid-zone` follow where the map has them.
  - `CoordinateMapper.GetMapGeoreference` computes it. The transform is where `GetRealWorld`, which world files are written with, puts the map's origin and unit vectors. It gives null for a map without real-world coordinates. OCAD's grid 1000, which says that a map has no coordinate system, identifies none.
  - `RealWorldCoords.CoordinateSystemEpsg` gives the EPSG code however the map names its system: a code it was given, or the code of its OCAD grid. `Epsg` keeps holding only a given code, which `OpenMapperExport` writes as such.
- **The EPSG codes of OCAD's grids (G-3).** A row of `OcadToProj4Projection`'s table carries an EPSG code where PROJ's definition of the code and the row's Proj4 string have the same unit and put every point of the code's area of use within 1 mm of each other; a deprecated code gives way to the replacement EPSG names, where that one passes. 624 of the 642 rows carry one so. Four grids more follow EPSG definitions withdrawn as wrong, which place points 29 m to 170 km from EPSG's corrected systems, and name those withdrawn codes (see deprecated EPSG codes), so 628 name a code. `SvgGeoreference/README.md` lists the others and why, and `check_with_pyproj.py` beside it repeats the check.
- **The loop's improvements.** On Oct 23 @Pauli agreed to all the loop's improvements in the task list, fonts in the repository included, leaving their order to the executor. He also asked whether the SVG should be measured against a vector PDF baseline. A PDF rendered to pixels would add a third rasterizer and check what the Skia references already check, so the comparison stays with Skia; vector checks go where pixels are blind: where Chromium puts each character, the geometry of each SVG against its drawing calls (V-10), and the CMYK values the exports write.

The SVG keeps the shaped positions, and `fix/skia-subpixel-glyph-positions`, which `feature/svg-text` merged, turns subpixel positioning on in the Skia target, with the baselines it moves, so that the text references show the glyphs where the shaping puts them. - **Characters by their positions.** The target writes each cluster's start as `x`. Chromium places a character given a position by itself, a mark apart from its letter, so a list of positions ends at the first cluster of several characters, and the clusters after it are placed by a `tspan` of their own; since phase 7 it ends at a character outside the Basic Multilingual Plane too, which Firefox counts as two (see the design's "Firefox and WebKit"). Shifts from a viewer's own advances, `dx`, which the target wrote first, carried Chromium's rounding of each advance, by up to 0.04 pixels a glyph at some scales, along the line: the last of 40 characters lay 0.6 pixels from where Skia's shaping put it. Absolute positions also free the document from every other viewer's rounding.
- **Chromium's hinting.** The headless shell installed in the cloud sessions is a wrapper that starts Chromium with `--font-render-hinting=none`, `--disable-lcd-text` and `--force-color-profile=srgb`; the one GitHub's runners install starts it with none of them, and there Chromium hinted the glyphs and moved them by up to three pixels, which failed 64 text cases on Oct 24. `BrowserRenderer` (`ChromiumRenderer` until Nov 12) and `SvgGallery/render-cases.mjs` pass the three flags themselves, and the loop and a local run agree on every case whose fonts are the tests' own.
- **The fallback case's fonts.** fontfallback draws the characters its fonts lack in faces that the platform's font fallback finds among the machine's own fonts (`FontFallbackResolver`, which leaves registered fonts out on purpose), so its reference depended on the machine. - **Fonts and determinism.** On Oct 24 @Pauli required that a document whose fonts are embedded never be drawn in a font of the viewer's system: the reader must get the signal the map's maker intended, not only the tests. Each embedded face therefore has a family of its own, the font's own face under the map's family (T-6) and each fallback face under its own name, numbered when a family, weight and style repeat, and a text writes each run a fallback face draws as a `tspan` naming that face, where `unicode-range` had left the choice to the browser. - **The pixel tolerance (F-1).** `PixelTolerance` holds the rule, so that a case can carry a tolerance of its own, as V-3 foresees for fine evenly spaced lines; the check of Chromium's rendering on whole pixels keeps an exact one. Beyond the image both images count as white paper, which keeps shapes cut by the image's edge fair.
  - A pixel of Chromium's image passes when each color channel lies within the range that channel takes in the reference within one pixel, give or take 4 levels, and the same holds the other way round.
  - Inside a shape this asks for a match within 4 levels; along an edge it lets any shade between the colors meeting there pass.
  - Where the edges of two shapes meet in one pixel, each rasterizer mixes their colors with the one beneath its own way, which can fall outside every neighbour's range. Such a pixel, with an edge within reach, passes when the two images, each blurred by a Gaussian of one pixel with white paper beyond them, differ there in every channel by at most 40 levels and by at most 0.4 of the contrast around it. The contrast is the widest range a channel takes in either image within the blur's reach of 4 pixels.

  In the 106 drawn cases it was chosen over, pixels differ by up to 191 levels along edges. In 57 of them, pixels where edges meet lie up to 102 levels outside the range; there the blurred images differ by at most 34.6 levels, and by at most 0.36 of the contrast. With errors planted in Chromium's images, the rule fails all 106 cases shifted by two pixels, the faintest included; all 106 with features two pixels wider; 96 of the 97 with a solid feature when their smallest one is removed; and 93 of the 105 with ink to change when their ink is six levels redder. The other 12 hold their ink in strokes and dots too thin to have an inside. The bitmap cases fail with every planted error.

  A drawing that draws text is held to `PixelTolerance.Text`, the same rule with 80 edge levels instead of 40 (T-10). Chromium shades the edges of glyphs, and of the frames drawn around them, otherwise than Skia does: over the 81 text cases, with the references' glyphs at their shaped subpixel positions and no system font but the sentinel, no pixel lies outside the range away from an edge, but at edges the blurred images differ by up to 75 levels (frametext3), darker at plain glyphs and lighter too at framed ones, and by at most 0.37 of the contrast. With the planted errors, the rule fails all 81 shifted and all 81 wider, 63 of the 80 with a feature removed and 62 of the 81 redder; every rule of its form that passes the 81 passes 15 of those removals, whose blurred differences are no larger than real ones. - **Colours for Scribus and Inkscape.** On Nov 4 @Pauli chose, from what the readers of `SvgColourReaders/` found, to write each style as a `style` attribute instead of a class of the `<style>` sheet, which Scribus 1.6 ignores, and to embed a 516-byte device-CMYK ICC profile behind the name `cmyk`, without which Inkscape shows no CMYK channels; on Nov 5, to put a shared style on the outermost group whose elements share it rather than the innermost, though Inkscape then shows a colour on a symbol's group rather than nearer each object. A paint keeps its two declarations, sRGB first, so that Chromium draws as before; map-sized exports came out 2.5 to 14 % smaller. A profile sampled from Purple Pen's SWOP table would bring Inkscape's preview within about 4.5 levels of Purple Pen's colours, against about 35, but it derives from Adobe's profile, whose licence forbids modifying it.
- **The Skia tests' fonts.** On Nov 5 @Pauli kept upstream's tests and baselines as they are: the Skia tests stay on the machine's fonts, which the workflow pins (`.github/ci/environment.json` and the fonts the job installs), and do not move onto the fixed font set, which stays the SVG tests' alone. A text case that draws a family the job installs in another face is a fault of how the fonts are installed or found, to be fixed in the workflow, or in this fork's own code if the evidence shows it there.
- **The fonts the tests install.** Six Skia text cases (TestWest, TestWest11, ArialNarrow, FramingText1 to 3) now and then drew Arial Narrow as Arial. The install step stopped the font cache service, but installing a font starts it again partway through the copy, and a font set built from a half-installed store can reach a test process minutes later. `ci/installed-fonts-visible` (71a29e0) keeps the service disabled until every file is in, then checks that DirectWrite has every face the registry names, waiting up to two minutes; the failure came in 1 of 16 copies of the job before it and in none of 24 after, nor in a full run since. Of Arial Narrow's two versions, which carry the same names, Windows' registry had kept 2.20, which DirectWrite and Skia drew, and 2.40 had reached GDI alone; WPF's baselines need 2.40 and every other group draws alike with either, so `.github/ci/fonts.json` declares 2.40 (`ARIALN*.TTF`) alone. On Nov 5 @Pauli decided that fonts no test needs come out of windows-fonts: 14 files stay, and 26 went, once a full run showed that no baseline moves.
