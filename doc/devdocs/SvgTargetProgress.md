## How to pick the work up

- **Loop run.** Rebuild `svg/svg-target` as one merge commit of `origin/base` and `origin/feature/svg-target` (`git merge-tree --write-tree`, then `git commit-tree`), push it with `--force-with-lease`, follow the run through the GitHub API, and read `summary.md`, `failures.md` and `pending.tsv` on `svg-results/svg-target`. Its `measurements/`, `references/`, `differences/` and `renderings/` hold the numbers and images of the comparison.
- **Local run.** In a cloud session, install the .NET 10 SDK with `dotnet-install.sh --channel 10.0 --azure-feed https://dotnetcli.blob.core.windows.net/dotnet`, build `src/MapModel/Map_Svg.Tests`, install Playwright's pinned Chromium with `bin/Debug/net10.0/playwright.ps1 install --only-shell chromium` (PowerShell from its GitHub release), and run `dotnet test` with `SVG_LOOP_RESULTS` naming a results directory. The run reproduces the loop's measurements and reference images byte for byte. The environment's proxy lets through the hosts this needs: api.nuget.org, dotnetcli.blob.core.windows.net, cdn.playwright.dev and storage.googleapis.com.
- **Gallery.** Keep it current: publish whenever there is something new to show, about every 30 minutes of work, and at the end of each step. Clone `svg-gallery` and check out `svg-results/svg-target`. The loop keeps Chromium's image of a case only when it falls outside the tolerance, so render the cases to show with `SvgGallery/render-cases.mjs` beside this file, with `CHROMIUM_EXECUTABLE` naming the headless shell of the tests' Playwright package; its images match the loop's pixel for pixel. Then run `SvgGallery/make-gallery-step.py` on the checkout and the `svg_reference` of the commit the loop ran, naming the entry and the cases to show: it writes the entry's folder and prints the figures and census that `index.html` takes into `LOG` and `STANDING`. Move the course's states on, look at the page with `SvgGallery/look-at-gallery.mjs`, and publish it with `SvgGallery/publish-svg-gallery.sh`, which replaces `svg-gallery`'s commit and puts the gallery under `svg/` on `gh-pages`. A `wip/` branch made before `build.yml` copied `svg-gallery` into the site publishes without it, until the next publish from `base`, `dev` or a newer branch.
- **Full run.** A change is finished only after a `wip/` branch rooted at `base`, with the feature merged in, has run the Windows suite (DL-10). That suite stops on a machine whose Windows version or system fonts differ from `.github/ci/environment.json`; a run started by hand with `machine_check` set to `report` tests anyway, and the stopped job's log prints the record that machine would need.

## Decisions

- **The structured interface.** `IStructuredGraphicsTarget`, in `Graphics2D`, extends `IGraphicsTarget`. Its layout calls exist: `BeginPart` and `EndPart` around a part of a drawing, which the document defines once; `BeginMap` and `EndMap` around a labeled map, nested for a template; and `AddPage(paperSize, mapArea, mapToPage, partKeys)`, made outside parts. Step 3 adds `BeginColorRun(colorKey, position, name, color, overprint)` with `EndColorRun`, and `BeginObject(objectKey, symbolKey, symbolId, symbolName)` with `EndObject`; step 4 adds `SetGeoreference(mapKey, MapGeoreference)`. @Pauli approved these calls.
- **Shared code.** `IdentityComparer` lives in `Graphics2D`, from where every graphics target, the SVG target too, keys its resources by identity (C-4, C-6).
- **The document.** Parts are groups with ids in `<defs>`. A page is a group carrying the page's transform and a clip of its map area, with a `<use>` for each part it shows. Pages after the first lie to its right, 10 mm apart, and the root has the first page's size in millimetres. The metadata's root is `npp:document version="1"`, holding an `npp:map` with the number and label of each map, whose groups carry `data-m`.
- **Numbers.** Coordinates are whole steps of 0.001 mm, rounded half up as `floor(x + 0.5)`, which every runtime does alike (P-6). A transform carries the digits that the reach of its content needs: within half a step for its translation, and within 0.45 of a step for its linear part, shared between angle (0.2), scale (0.2) and shear (0.05).
- **The text.** Each path command takes its shortest form; arc flags keep their separators, which SVG 1.1 readers need, and transform functions follow each other without one. Classes and ids are named in the order they are first used, the shortest names first. Groups are tidied before writing: empty ones dropped, those carrying nothing dissolved, an only child folded into its parent where the order of transform and clip allows, and adjacent groups with equal attributes merged, unless they blend.
- **Colors.** `icc-color()` names the profile `cmyk`, declared as `<color-profile name="cmyk"/>` with no ICC profile behind it. The sRGB comes from the converter passed in; the comparison passes `DefaultColorConverter`, which converts as the Skia references do.
- **Anti-aliasing.** Browsers anti-alias by default, so a group carries `shape-rendering` only where the mode changes.
- **Errors.** A call the target does not implement yet raises `Svg_UnsupportedCallException`, which makes a comparison case pending; a call out of turn raises an `InvalidOperationException` that names it.
- **Pens.** A pen is a class: `fill:none`, its paint, its width in steps, then its cap and join where they differ from SVG's defaults of butt, miter and a miter limit of 4 (D-5, S-9). Skia bevels every corner under a miter limit of 1 or less, which SVG would take for an error, so such a pen is written with a bevel join. A width of 0, Skia's hairline of one device pixel at any scale, has no unit in a document and is an unsupported call, as the clipped miter join is. A pen made from a brush strokes with the brush's paint (D-6).
- **Shapes.** Lines, polylines, polygons, rectangles and paths are written as paths, ellipses as circles or ellipses. An arc's angles are degrees turning from the x axis towards the y axis, as Skia's are; it is written as an arc command, and an arc of a full turn or more as a circle, as Skia draws it as a closed oval (D-4). A path's parts become its commands, a line or curve with no start before it beginning at the origin as in Skia, and a path with nothing in it draws nothing (D-3).
- **Clips.** A clip is a group with a `clip-path`. The document defines each clip path once for all equal clips, the page's among them, since a clip path is read in the user space of what refers to it. The union of rectangles is one subpath per rectangle, each turning clockwise, under the nonzero rule (D-2).
- **The map's structure.** `IStructuredGraphicsTarget` has `BeginColorRun(colorKey, position, name, color, overprint)` with `EndColorRun`, and `BeginObject(objectKey, symbolKey, symbolId, symbolName)` with `EndObject`. To a target that implements it, `Map.DrawColor` opens a run of a color of the color table before its first object and closes it after its last, and brackets each object with its symbol; the image and layout layers, which are not colors of the table, open no run, so their objects sit in their map outside any run. `Map.DrawTemplates` labels each template with its file name (M-3 to M-5). A run is a group carrying `data-c`, the color's position in its map's color table. Consecutive objects of one symbol share a group carrying `data-s`, the symbol's number in its map's symbol table, counted from 1 in the order the map's symbols first begin an object; a call that closes or begins something around the group closes it, once its last object has ended. Each element carries `data-o`, its object's number, counted across the document from 1 in the order objects are first drawn, so an object drawn in several runs has one number (M-6, S-11). Each `npp:map` holds, in table order, an `npp:color` for each color its runs drew (`position`, `name`, `cmyk` with the digits of `icc-color()`, and `overprint="1"` when it overprints), then an `npp:symbol` for each symbol its objects drew (`n`, `id`, `name`, and `colors`, the positions of the colors its objects were drawn in) (M-7). The structure adds 21 % to the SVG of the 101 drawn cases, most of it the object numbers.
- **Pattern brushes.** `CreatePatternBrush` returns a target that draws the tile as the target draws, with keys of its own and at its creator's intensity, and refuses structure calls; `FinishBrush` makes the tile a brush of its creator, and a pen made from it strokes with the pattern (D-6, D-7). The document defines the pattern in the user space of what it paints: `x`, `y` and a `viewBox` put the tile's middle on the origin, in the tile's own coordinates, and `patternTransform` turns it by the brush's angle, as the Skia target repeats its tile. The bitmap size, a hint to raster targets, is not used. A style refers to its pattern as `url(#)` until the document is written, when the pattern's id is named in the order patterns are first used, and only patterns that a style paints with are written. Equal patterns are separate definitions until S-12 shares them, in phase 6.
- **Blending.** Darken is the only mode, written as a group with `mix-blend-mode:darken` that never merges with its neighbours; the map opens it around the run of one overprinted color, so the group blends that run as a whole (D-8). Any other mode is an unsupported call.
- **The comparison.** Each case is one map in one part on one page whose millimetres are the image's pixels, and Chromium shows the SVG through an HTML page that sizes it to the image. The census records each case with `StructuredRecordingGraphicsTarget`, a `RecordingGraphicsTarget` that also records the structure calls the map code makes, through `RecordStructure`, and plays them back onto a target that keeps structure (DL-4); they are not drawing calls, so the census leaves them out. Reference images are rendered by the Skia target from the case's own drawing, anti-aliased on white (V-9), with its clips anti-aliased too, as Chromium draws every clip path whatever its shape-rendering; a missing one goes to the results' `references/`, from where it is committed into `src/MapModel/TestFiles/svg_reference`.
- **The pixel tolerance (F-1).** A pixel of Chromium's image passes when each color channel lies within the range that channel takes in the reference within one pixel, give or take 4 levels, and the same holds the other way round; beyond the image both count as white paper, which keeps shapes cut by the image's edge fair. Inside a shape this asks for a match within 4 levels; along an edge it lets any shade between the colors meeting there pass. Where the edges of two shapes meet in one pixel, each rasterizer mixes their colors with the one beneath its own way, which can fall outside every neighbour's range; such a pixel, with an edge within reach, passes when the two images, each blurred by a Gaussian of one pixel with white paper beyond them, differ there by at most 40 levels in every channel. In the first 99 cases, pixels differ by up to 191 levels along edges; in 53 of them, pixels where edges meet lie up to 60 levels outside the range, and there the blurred images differ by at most 34.2. With errors planted in Chromium's images, the rule fails 97 of the 99 cases shifted by two pixels, all 99 with features two pixels wider, 95 with their smallest solid feature removed, and 86 of the 98 with ink to change with their ink six levels redder. What passes is ink in strokes too thin to have an inside, and the faintest drawings: decreasesymbols, whose darkest pixel lies 24 levels from white, passes even shifted. `PixelTolerance` holds the rule, so that a case can carry a tolerance of its own, as V-3 foresees for fine evenly spaced lines; the check of Chromium's rendering on whole pixels keeps an exact one.
