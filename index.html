Translation Logbook

NotPurplePen, a fork of the orienteering course-setting program Purple Pen, completes the translations of its 26 translated languages, one key group at a time, each for all languages at once. After each group, this log shows what each language still lacks and what the group brought. The translators are language models; each translation names the model and its effort setting.

Key group MainFrame.4

Nov 16, 2026. The fourth part of the main window: the Course, Item, Reports and Help menus of the menu bar, with the submenu Leg Flagging of the Item menu, and the four items of the hidden Debug menu of the WinForms application (Symbol Browser, Description Browser, Control Tester, Map Tester), which the Avalonia application does not show. claude-opus-5-5 at effort max made every line below, and each entry says so in its comment.

  • Translated: 115 lines, in 13 languages. ru lacked the whole group, and nl all of it but eight items of the Item menu. sl, nn-NO and ro lacked the newer items of the Course and Item menus (Duplicate Course, Course Order, Relay Team Variations, Delete Fork/Loop, Remove Gap, Change Line Appearance, Stretch) and Make a Donation; sl lacked About and the Debug items too. 11 languages lacked Stretch. Each new item of a menu has an access key that no other item of its menu uses. The items of the Reports menu are also the titles of their reports. nl calls the course "route" and the leg "been", as its terms do, and writes "estafetteteam" as one word. The Translated Help item of nl and ru names the language, as fi and nb-NO do, because it shows only where a help in the language exists.
  • Not needed: none.
  • Corrected: 122 lines, in 22 languages, most of them after terms.csv.
  • da, 29 lines. Its course was "kursus" (a course of study), its control "kontrol" (a check), its leg "ben" (a leg of the body), its taping "udflagning" (no Danish word), its fork the English "fork", its bend "buk" (a he-goat), its Item menu "Vare" (merchandise) and its event a "hændelse" (an incident). Its Competitor Load was "Konkurrent Læs" (a rival's cartload), and its Control and Leg Load "Kontrol og læs" (check and read). They are now "bane", "post", "stræk", "afmærket", "gafling", "knæk", "Element" and "løb".
  • The leg. tr's leg was "bacak", a leg of the body; it is now "ayak". cs's leg between two controls was "úsek", a leg of a relay; it is now "postup". The Italian legs were "frazioni", the legs of a relay; they are now "tratte". uk's nominative was the Russian "перегон".
  • Wrong meanings. The Italian Leg Flagging was the changeover zone of a relay. Its Web Site item said "Purple Pen Help (Translated)", and its Translated Help item was English. sl's Change Text was Delete Text. de's Change Displayed Courses named another thing, and its Leg Lengths were the lengths of courses. hu's and tr's Change Line Appearance changed a line of text. fr's Control Cross-reference was a cross-check. fr, pt and pt-BR named the Control and Leg Load with the verb that loads a file ("Charger", "Carregar"). tr's relay was an electrical relay ("Röle"), and its Competitor Load meant "load a rival". pt-BR's Course Order was "course and order", and its Event Audit audited a course. pt's flagged beginning and end were of a course. et's Entire Leg Flagged was a command. zh-CN's competitor was a rival (竞争对手), and its leg a course of study (课程). zh-TW's load was the loading of a file (載入).
  • English. cs's Stretch, fi's Make a Donation and Debug items, the bend of nb-NO and nn-NO, da's Symbol Browser and Support/Bug Reporting, and pt's Support were English.
  • Grammar and typos. sl and the Italian put the English word for word ("Proga Povzetek", "Tekmovalec Obremenitev", "Percorso Sommario"). tr's compounds lacked their suffix ("Parkur Özet", "Etkinlik Denetim"), and "Ekle_Kıvrım" and "Ekle_Açıklık" lacked a space. Typos: cs "zančení" and "Přida", et "järjekod" and "Purpe", uk "Довжити", ro "Fruzărire", "Adaugare" and "si", pl "Złóź", fr "DupliquerCircuit", a space in zh-CN and two in pt-BR. bg and ro did not agree in gender. The Italian "Duplicata" was a noun. nb-NO, nl, hu and sl split compounds or wrote capitals. nn-NO wrote bokmål ("merket", "hjemmeside", "utnyttelse").
  • Left empty: none.
  • terms.csv: five cells were corrected with the group, and terms-decisions.tsv records each: nl's Relay Team and Relay Team Variations, tr's Change Line Appearance and Control Cross-reference, and da's Delete Fork/Loop.

Doubts:

  • ru's Course menu takes the access key "и" ("Д_истанция"), as "д" is its Add menu. Its Help menu, in MainFrame.5, keeps "с" for "Справка".
  • nl's Item menu is "Voorwerp", the term of terms.csv, from the language's report column. "Element" or "Object" is the more usual name of such a menu.
  • nl's Competitor Load is "Drukte per route", after its term "Drukte" and its Leg load "Drukte per been".
  • The Italian keeps "Controllo" and "Controlli" in the items that are otherwise right (Control Tester, Change Control Codes), as MainFrame.2 and MainFrame.3 do. The items that the group corrected take the term "punti".
  • The Italian Remove Gap "Rimuovi Tratta Vuota" stays. "Tratta" is also the language's leg, but in Italian it is any stretch of a line.
  • de's Add Bend and Remove Bend keep "Eckpunkt", which terms.csv finds ambiguous with Add Corner. The menu shows the two items together.
  • Some existing items keep a form against terms.csv, as they are not wrong: cs's Relay Team Variations "Úseky štafet", the Italian About "Riguardo A Purple Pen", uk's Change Displayed Courses "Відобразити на дистанціях", and hu's "Váltó csapat Variációk".
  • Several menus had an access key twice before this group (the Item menus of bg, cs, de, es, et, fi, hu, pl and sv, the Reports menus of bg and sl, and several Help menus). The group leaves those of the lines that it does not change, and adds no new one.
  • Some items have no access key: no item of the zh-CN and zh-TW menus has one, and the Control and Leg Load of nb-NO and nn-NO had none. check.py warns of them. Items that are longer than the English have room, as a menu widens.
